What We Can Work With

Areas of Focus & Deep Support

People come to therapy for many different reasons. Whether your feelings feel clear and sharp or vague and heavy, all of you is welcome here.

01.

Anxiety, Panic & Overwhelm

When constant worry, physical tension, or panic leaves you exhausted, we gently untangle the triggers beneath the nervous system and build tools for steady ground.

02.

Low Mood & Depression

Feeling flat, hopeless, or disconnected from life. Rather than forcing toxic positivity, we explore what your depression might be trying to communicate about your needs.

03.

Trauma & Painful Memories

Carrying childhood wounds, emotional neglect, or single-incident shocks. We work safely and at your pace, ensuring you remain anchored in the present without overwhelm.

04.

Relationship Patterns

Repeating cycles with partners, boundary struggles with family, or feelings of isolation. We look at relational templates and help you build genuine closeness with healthy limits.

05.

Grief, Loss & Bereavement

The death of a loved one, the ending of a marriage, health changes, or loss of identity. Grief takes its own time; here it is honoured and given full room to breathe.

06.

Life Transitions & Meaning

Midlife questioning, retirement, career changes, or a quiet feeling that "something is missing". We tune into your inner values and what genuinely matters to your soul.

Integrative Methodology

How We Work Together

Integrative transpersonal psychotherapy combines established psychological frameworks with creative and soulful insight.

Relational & Person-Centred

Healing happens in relation. The safety, trust, and openness between therapist and client provide a secure base to explore vulnerable truths without fear of rejection.

Creative & Metaphorical Expressions

Sometimes words alone cannot capture what you feel. In the studio, I have paper, paints, pastels, and sand trays available. If you feel like you are "underwater" or "in a fog", we can work directly with those powerful images.

Soulful & Transpersonal Perspective

Drawing on the work of Thomas Moore and James Hillman, we attend to symptoms not merely as flaws to fix, but as voices of the soul asking for closer attention, meaning, and care.
